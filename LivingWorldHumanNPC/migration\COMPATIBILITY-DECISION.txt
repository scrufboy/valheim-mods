Decision:
- Keep registered prefab LWC_HumanScout_v1. Do not rename, destroy/recreate the
  saved actor, change its ZDO ID, or require a new world/site/lab.
- Keep world LWC_HumanLab_v1 and player profile LWC_Tester.
- The visual contract remains native-human-v1, which denotes the same native
  human visual/attachment arrangement, not a license to reinterpret the root.
- Add independent composition revision human-components-v1. No existing saved
  revision is silently upgraded on Start, discovery, follow, appearance or load.
- Exact known legacy tuple (visual=native-human-v1, identity=LWC_HumanScout_v1,
  lab tag=npc, no composition) is eligible for explicit lwtest migrate only.
- Until the command succeeds, autonomous companion behavior is gated. The
  candidate uses the new component root at load; it does NOT secretly retain or
  instantiate the old creature root while waiting for consent.
- Migration writes only two custom fields: composition origin first and new
  composition revision last. The native-human-v1 field is not overwritten.
- The origin is legacy-0.2.x-native-human-v1; fresh explicit spawns instead get
  fresh-human-components-v1. A missing/unknown/conflicting revision or identity
  fails closed and is never reset as a fresh actor.
- A partial legacy origin write before the composition commit remains gated and
  may be retried with an explicit migrate command. Missing current origin or
  unknown future compositions are refused, not repaired speculatively.
- Migration also asks the companion layer to write its separate initial Hold
  order bound to the invoking player. Unknown existing order data is not erased.
- Fresh metadata is allowed only on the exact new instance returned directly by
  the explicit spawn command, with world, local player, owner and prefab checks.
  Empty metadata on a loaded actor is NOT evidence of a new spawn.
- No claim that rollback is safe after using 0.3.0. This remains a review candidate
  and should not be installed over a live session or substituted into frozen 0.2.2.
