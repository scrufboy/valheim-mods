# Living World C.O.R.E. — release photography plan

Owner and original work: Brian / scrufboy. Date: 2026-10-20.
Status: editorial production plan; screenshots and runtime support remain unverified.

## Identity and editorial boundary

**Living World Studio** remains the working utility name. **Portrait Hall Door**
is the working entrance label. Final package and entrance names await the existing
Valheim Mod Story Research naming track; this document does not approve a new name.
The timber doorway belongs visually beside ordinary Valheim construction. Its
description can say: “Enter a controlled room for portraits and release artwork.”
The chroma room is a production tool, not a new realm or an ordinary part of canon.

Use **Living World C.O.R.E.** in the release family lockup. Spell out
**Characters, Origins, Roles & Encounters** on the first overview card. Credit
**Created by Brian / scrufboy**. Preserve the distinct **Formidable NPC Framework**
technical identity and **FORMIDABLE — Odin's Friends & Foes** narrative identity
where each is actually relevant. Do not rename companion classes or enemy families
for a photograph. Published faction labels must match the currently approved
release content, not an earlier naming proposal.

## Capture lanes

| Lane | What it supplies | Public caption rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Studio portrait | Clean subject, equipment, silhouette and lineup plates | Identify staged portrait when context could imply live behavior |
| Studio scenery | Render-only vanilla tree, bush, furniture and architecture dressing | Identify staged scene; scenery placement does not prove gathering, work or settlement simulation |
| Composite artwork | Keyed subject over an original in-world background plate | Label “Composite promotional artwork” |
| Genuine gameplay | Combat, defense, travel, return, work and recovery as actually implemented | State observed behavior and candidate version; do not use staged poses as proof |
| Narrative concept | Future reclamation scenes whose mechanics are not yet implemented | Label “Narrative concept — feature not yet implemented” |

The camera may show high-resolution in-game detail, but resolution is a capture
property: do not claim that the utility replaces Valheim textures or supplies an
HD asset pack. Keep native shapes and materials recognizable.

## A coherent reclamation image sequence

1. **The abandoned threshold:** genuine village background plate, preserving its
   actual state before any staging.
2. **The danger faced:** genuine enemy and encounter footage; select a readable
   still showing the established enemy identity.
3. **The return:** use actual arrival footage when implemented. Until then, a
   composite of adult villagers/companions is explicitly narrative concept art.
4. **The living hearth:** capture accepted town activities in the world; use a
   staged group portrait as the release's closing image.

This sequence supports the return-and-reclamation story while keeping current
mechanics distinguishable from the narrative destination.

## Repeatable capture session

Record game/mod candidate versions, studio version, graphics preset, resolution,
camera mode/FOV and subject roster before a session. Save one neutral reference
frame before changing palette, props or poses. Keep a camera marker for the
individual portrait and a second for the full party. Use the same framing across
friendlies and enemies where a size comparison is intended.

Start with the ordinary first/third-person camera. Hide HUD, panel and pad markers
for capture while retaining a documented way to reopen controls. An optional
camera tool may add compositions only after compatibility testing; no photograph
should imply that it is required to use the studio.

For OBS, keep an unkeyed capture source and a separate keyed presentation source.
Retain the unkeyed original so keying can be redone. Set capture/output size to
the chosen actual game resolution and avoid upscaling being described as native
detail. Record brief idle and pose loops for moving media, plus still captures
for release cards. Capture audio separately when useful; studio silence is not
evidence of game audio behavior.

Choose green for most subjects, blue when green foliage/clothing/effects would be
erased, and neutral for reflective, transparent or difficult-edge subjects.
Keying settings are per shot: do not force one aggressive filter across hair,
antlers, bows, shields, foliage and cloth. Check edges at full resolution against
both a dark and a light test background. Avoid cutting off weapons, antlers or
feet. Capture foliage separately when it complicates subject edges.

Vanilla scenery inside the studio should be deliberately selected and bounded.
Tree/bush dressing can support a forest portrait; furniture and wooden walls can
support a hearth tableau. Photograph render-only dressing as dressing. Live
fire, particles, damage, harvesting and world simulation remain outside the
quiet studio's default promise. Use genuine world plates for those effects.

## Masters, exports and credits

Maintain an unaltered original, an editable composite project when used, and a
final export. Suggested filename pattern:
`LWC_<release>_<subject>_<shot>_<studio|gameplay|composite|concept>_<take>`.
Use a capture log for roster, loadouts, palette, props, poses and processing.
Prepare a wide release banner, a square card, a full-resolution portrait and a
short landscape showcase clip from approved masters. Crop deliberately rather
than stretching a team lineup into several aspect ratios.

Keep the visual system restrained: Valheim imagery first, warm timber/iron framing,
one consistent title treatment, readable captions and a small Brian / scrufboy
credit. Chroma green is a production surface, not the release brand color.
Do not add an engine endorsement, official Valheim logo treatment or third-party
asset ownership claim. Credit installed third-party content when a specific shot
requires it, and keep such dependencies distinguishable from original work.

## Handoff and acceptance

The technical track owns door/room placement, rendering, prop safety, transfer,
subject authorization and recovery. The story track owns approved names, canon
copy and narrative claims. This plan is the editorial handoff between them; it
does not claim that another existing chat has received a message or approved it.

Before publishing: identify every image's capture lane; verify visible equipment
and names against the candidate; check keying edges and color spill; retain
originals; confirm every gameplay caption has supporting footage; and mark
unimplemented reclamation scenes as narrative concepts. A polished team photo
can sell the characters without being presented as proof of their AI.
